Three Types of Product Managers
Internal PM

🏢 Internal Tools

  • Builds tools for internal employees
  • Stakeholders are inside the company
  • Lower risk — smaller user base
  • Good entry-level PM role
  • Heavy on project management
B2B PM

🤝 Business-to-Business

  • Builds for other companies
  • Heavily influenced by sales teams
  • Smaller user base, higher deal value
  • Tighter deadlines tied to deals
  • Good introduction to PM
B2C PM

👥 Business-to-Consumer

  • Builds for millions of users
  • Multiple platforms (Web, iOS, Android)
  • High uncertainty, high pressure
  • Extensive user testing & A/B testing
  • Most challenging but most rewarding

Product Lifecycle — The 4 Phases

Revenue Over Time — Product Lifecycle Curve

1. Introduction

Product enters market. Little competition. Business loses money. Only early adopters buy.

2. Growth

Market acceptance. Sales rise. Few competitors appear. Company invests in improvements.

3. Maturity

Sales peak. Competitors flood the market. Difficult to maintain competitive edge.

4. Decline

Sales diminish. Market saturation. Product may be phased out or sunset.

Phases 1–4: Building

  • Conceive: Collect user problems, brainstorm solutions, identify focus areas
  • Plan: Market research, business case, customer interviews, roadmap
  • Develop: Timelines, user stories, specs, engineering estimations
  • Iterate: MVP → early feedback → Alpha → Beta testing

Phases 5–7: Scaling & Deciding

  • Launch: Coordinate with marketing, legal, PR, sales for public release
  • Steady State: Collect metrics, optimize, continue marketing
  • Maintain or Kill: Evaluate ROI — if underperforming, sunset the product gracefully

  • Don't pitch — listen and learn
  • Ask open-ended questions

  • Dig deeper with "why?"
  • Don't lead the witness
  • Look for patterns across interviews

⚙️ Talking to Engineers

  • Tell what, not how
  • Give context — the "why"
  • Trust their estimates
  • Include them early
  • Be their shield
  • Credit them

🎨 Talking to Designers

  • Bring them in early
  • Share user research
  • Provide problems, not solutions
  • Collaborate visually
  • Give specific, constructive feedback

👔 Talking to Executives

  • Be data-driven
  • Be concise
  • Tell a story
  • Anticipate questions
  • Align with company goals
  • Regular updates

📅 First 90 Days as a New PM

Days 1–30: LEARN

Understand the product, meet everyone, review data & metrics, learn the business model, listen more than talk.

Days 30–60: CONTRIBUTE

Identify quick wins, build relationships, participate in planning, make small improvements.

Days 60–90: LEAD

Take ownership, drive initiatives, present recommendations, set longer-term vision.
